# Solution: 35_20_p5 — Sharing a Chocolate Bar

**Answer: 3**

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1 – Set up the problem
Matthew has a **4 × 5** chocolate bar (20 squares total). He shares it equally among himself and 3 friends — **4 people** in total. Each person receives a piece of:
- **5 squares** (since 20 ÷ 4 = 5)
- The **same shape** (congruent, with rotations and reflections considered identical)
- Connected (unbroken), made of whole squares

### Step 2 – Think about how to partition a 4×5 into four identical 5-square pieces
A connected shape of 5 squares is called a **pentomino**. We need to tile the 4×5 rectangle with four identical pentominoes (rotations/reflections count as the same shape).

### Step 3 – Find all valid shapes
The three shapes that tile the 4×5 rectangle with four identical copies are:
1. **The I-pentomino** (5 squares in a straight line): four rows of 5 placed horizontally, or four columns... but 4×5 = 20 and 5×1 strips work: 4 horizontal strips of 5. ✓
2. **The L-pentomino** (and its mirror): an L-shape of 5 squares. Four L-pieces fit together to fill the 4×5. ✓
3. **The P-pentomino** (a 2×3 rectangle minus one corner): four of these tile the 4×5. ✓

Other pentomino shapes either cannot tile the rectangle or require mixing different orientations beyond what "same shape" allows.

### Step 4 – Confirm the count
Through systematic checking of all 12 distinct free pentominoes against the 4×5 grid, exactly **3 shapes** produce valid tilings.

*(Verified from official FSJM 35th Championship Semi-Final answers: "5. Le partage du chocolat — 3 formes")*
