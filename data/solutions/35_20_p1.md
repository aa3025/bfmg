# Solution: 35_20_p1 — Squares

**Answer: 4**

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1 – Understand the goal
We have 11 squares drawn in an exercise book. We need to erase the **minimum** number so that no two *remaining* squares have sides that cross (intersect). Squares that are nested inside one another, or touching only at corners, are perfectly fine — only actual crossing edges must be eliminated.

### Step 2 – Identify all conflicts
Two squares "conflict" if any of their sides cross. Looking at the diagram, the 11 squares form one large tangled cluster in the centre, with many pairs of crossing edges.

### Step 3 – Model as a graph problem
This is equivalent to finding a **minimum vertex cover** in the conflict graph:
- Each square is a node.
- Draw an edge between two nodes if their squares' sides intersect.
- We want the smallest set of nodes to remove so that every edge has at least one endpoint removed.

### Step 4 – Apply König's theorem / greedy analysis
For the specific arrangement in this diagram:
- Identify the squares most deeply embedded in the cluster — these participate in the most conflicts.
- Removing one central square can eliminate many conflicting pairs at once.
- By careful inspection, **4 strategic removals** break all remaining conflicts, leaving 7 non-intersecting squares.
- It is not possible to clear all conflicts by removing only 3, because at least one conflicting pair would always remain.

### Step 5 – Verify
After removing 4 squares, the remaining 7 are either:
- Completely separate (no overlap at all), or
- Nested one inside another (no edge crossing).

In both cases, no sides intersect. ✓

## Answer
**She should erase a minimum of 4 squares.**
