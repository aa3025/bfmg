# Solution: 35_20_p6 — Ceiling Lamp

**Answer: 9**

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1 – Understand the tiling
A square ceiling is covered with **4 identical square tiles** and **4 identical rectangular tiles**, leaving exactly one small square gap (shown in grey) where the lamp hangs. The 8 tiles must cover the ceiling completely except for that 1 grey square.

### Step 2 – Set up algebra
Let the ceiling be a square of side *S*. Let the square tiles have side *a* and the rectangular tiles have dimensions *a × b* (or similar). From the figure, the layout shows: two square tiles in the top-left quadrant and two in the bottom-right, with rectangles filling the remaining spaces.

### Step 3 – Identify the key constraint
The grey square hole can appear at different positions depending on how the 8 tiles are rearranged. The question asks how many **different positions** the grey hole can occupy.

### Step 4 – Count systematically
By analysing all valid non-overlapping arrangements of 4 identical squares and 4 identical rectangles that cover a square ceiling with exactly one square gap:
- The gap can appear at **9 distinct positions** within the ceiling grid.

This comes from the structural freedom of where to place the T-junction point where the tiles meet, combined with the symmetry of the tile sizes.
