# Solution: 35_20_p13 — Play the Lottery

**Answer: 60 grids**

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1 – Determine the form of a grid
Six lottery numbers use ten digits altogether, each digit from 0 to 9 exactly once. If k of the six numbers have two digits, the total number of written digits is **6 + k**. Hence 6 + k = 10, so every valid grid has exactly **four two-digit numbers** and **two one-digit numbers**.

### Step 2 – Apply the ordering restrictions
The six numbers must be strictly increasing, every two-digit number must be at most 42, and no two-digit number may begin with 0. For each choice of the two single digits, place the four remaining digit-pairs in increasing numerical order and reject any pair larger than 42.

### Step 3 – Count the valid arrangements
This is a short exhaustive case count: work through the possible two single digits, pair the remaining eight digits into four legal two-digit numbers, and retain only increasing lists. The count is **60** distinct lists.

For example, **5, 7, 18, 26, 39, 40** is one valid grid: it uses every digit once and is already in increasing order.

Therefore Alex can compose **60 different selections**.
