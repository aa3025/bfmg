# Solution: 35_20_p9 — Even Smaller

**Answer: 6 diamonds**

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1 – Find the area of the starting rectangle
The original rectangle measures **8 m by 4 m**, so its area is:

**8 × 4 = 32 m² = 3200 dm².**

### Step 2 – Track what happens at each inscription
Joining the midpoints of the sides of any rectangle or diamond makes a new figure with **half the area** of the previous one. Therefore the successive inscribed figures have areas:

| Inscribed figure | Shape | Area (m²) |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | diamond | 16 |
| 2 | rectangle | 8 |
| 3 | diamond | 4 |
| 4 | rectangle | 2 |
| 5 | diamond | 1 |
| 6 | rectangle | 1/2 |
| 7 | diamond | 1/4 |
| 8 | rectangle | 1/8 |
| 9 | diamond | 1/16 |
| 10 | rectangle | 1/32 |
| 11 | diamond | 1/64 |
| 12 | rectangle | 1/128 |

### Step 3 – Apply the stopping condition
Matilda stops when the newly inscribed figure has area less than **1 dm²**, which is **0.01 m²**.

The 11th inscribed figure has area **1/64 m² = 1.5625 dm²**, so she has not stopped yet. The 12th has area **1/128 m² = 0.78125 dm²**, which is less than 1 dm². So she stops after drawing the 12th figure.

### Step 4 – Count the diamonds
The diamonds are figures 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, and 11. Thus Matilda draws **6 diamonds** before the 12th figure makes her stop.
