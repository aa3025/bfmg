# Solution: 35_20_p8 — Jigsaw Puzzle Pieces

**Answer: 15**

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1 – Classify pieces by type
Every piece is a square with 4 sides. Each side is either **flat (F)**, **hollow (H)**, or **bump (B)**. Pieces are sorted into piles if they overlap perfectly when placed right-side-up **or upside-down** (i.e., two pieces are the same type if one is a rotation or reflection of the other).

There are three categories of pieces:
- **Corner pieces**: exactly 2 adjacent flat sides (the corner of the puzzle)
- **Edge pieces**: exactly 1 flat side (the border, non-corner)
- **Interior pieces**: no flat sides

### Step 2 – Count corner piece types
A corner piece has 2 fixed adjacent flat sides. The remaining 2 sides can each be H or B → 4 combinations: HH, HB, BH, BB. Since the piece can be flipped (reflecting the H/B pattern), HB and BH become equivalent.

**Corner types: HH, HB (=BH), BB → 3 types**

### Step 3 – Count edge piece types
An edge piece has 1 fixed flat side. The remaining 3 sides can each be H or B → 8 combinations. Flipping the piece mirrors left ↔ right. Self-symmetric patterns (same when mirrored): (H,H,H), (B,B,B), (H,B,H), (B,H,B) = 4. Non-symmetric pairs: (H,H,B)↔(B,H,H) and (H,B,B)↔(B,B,H) = 2 pairs.

**Edge types: 4 + 2 = 6 types**

### Step 4 – Count interior piece types
An interior piece has 4 sides each H or B → 16 patterns. Since the piece can be freely rotated (4 rotations) and flipped (reflected), we use the full symmetry group of the square (8 symmetries). Applying Burnside's lemma or direct enumeration:

**Interior types: 6 types**

(All-H, all-B, one-different, two-adjacent-different, two-opposite-different, three-different)

### Step 5 – Total
**3 + 6 + 6 = 15 piles**

*(Verified from official FSJM 35th Championship Semi-Final answers: "8. Les pièces de puzzle — 15 tas")*
